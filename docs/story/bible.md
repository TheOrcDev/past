# Past — Story bible

Status: draft, 2026-10-11, for plan 008. The core comes from the operator's
pick, "Thief of names" (`decisions.md` #43). Everything else here is Claude's
proposal, and it stands only once the operator signs off (board #10). Anything
marked **proposal** is a name or detail the operator can change without the
rest falling over.

This answers the open questions in `game-design.md` §13. The cast is in
`cast.md`, and the 50 rooms are in `atlas.md`.

## 1. The truth, in one paragraph

Unknown was **Ulric, High Magistrate** of the city. He kept order with the
**Ledger of Names**: a name written in it under his seal, then struck through,
is taken. Its owner forgets it, and every memory that hangs on it goes too. A
person without a name doesn't argue, doesn't organise and doesn't remember a
grievance, so the city grew obedient and, one taken name at a time, grey. The
woman he loved, his **dove**, sang the names of the taken in the cathedral, and
he took hers too. She stayed and forgave him anyway. In the end his own clerk,
the **Registrar**, struck Ulric's name from the Ledger. The crowd hanged a man
nobody could name, and his name was smashed off his grave on the night of the
burial. One letter survived: **U**.

## 2. The world's rules

1. **A name is never gone while one letter of it remains.** The U on the grave
   is why Unknown wakes. It is also the game's quiet promise: everything that
   was taken can come back.
2. **Color is memory.** Each name taken drained a little color, and when the
   last went, the city went grey (pillar 1, decision #24).
3. **The nameless don't change.** Time stopped for them on the night Ulric
   died. The clock tower stands at that hour. The citizens repeat the routines
   they no longer understand. They are grey **shades** of people, solid and
   alive, known by what they do: the Gravedigger, the Lamplighter, the Ferryman.
4. **Unknown is a struck man.** A person struck from the Ledger *and* killed
   becomes a shadow. He is black where the citizens are grey, because he is
   full of what he took.
5. **The flashbacks are stolen memories.** Every memory Ulric took went into
   him, not into the book. A place that a memory belongs to wakes it, and he
   sees it in color. Unknown believes they are his own memories ("And somehow,
   I was there to see it."). They are not, and that is the twist at the end of
   chapter 1. So no flashback can show anything after Ulric's death. The
   burial and his hanging are only ever told by the people who were there.
6. **Memories can be given back.** When Unknown finds whose memory he holds
   and gives it back, that person remembers their name, and color returns for
   good where they stand. This is the arc of every NPC, and it is how pillar
   1's escalation plays: the present gains color as he makes amends.
7. **The Ledger can return every name at once,** read aloud from the belfry
   while the great bell rings, because a bell carries to everyone who once had
   a name. Reading his own name last returns it to the city, not to him, and he
   fades.

## 3. People in the past

People in flashbacks are **memory figures**: glowing, featureless silhouettes
in warm light (`game-design.md` §6, board #5). Faces are what was taken, so
none are drawn. One figure in every memory is wrong: a man in a black robe
whose shape is burned out of the light, an absence. That is Ulric. Players see
him long before Unknown understands who he is.

**Unknown inside a flashback** (`game-design.md` §6, open) is an observer: his
shadow stands in the colored past and nobody sees him. In the single playable
memory where he *is* Ulric (chapter 3), he takes the burned-out figure's place.

## 4. The city

- **Name:** taken too. The citizens call it "the City". Its real name,
  **Aurelle** (proposal), is the last word spoken in the ending.
- **Districts:** the cemetery and the cathedral on the hill; the old town below
  them; the drowned quarter by the canals and the aqueduct; the upper city with
  the Hall of Records, the theatre and the gallows; and the cathedral's
  heights, with the dovecote and the belfry.
- **The flood:** the drowned quarter flooded because Ulric struck the names of
  the sluice-keepers who petitioned against him. With their names they forgot
  the gates, and the spring rains did the rest. It is the first crime Unknown
  finds that is unmistakably his.

## 6. Chapter outlines

### Prologue — The Grave (7 rooms: 2 built, 5 new)

Unknown wakes at his grave and reads the U. The **Gravedigger** dug that grave
and remembers the night: a veiled woman paid for the stone, and the
Registrar's men came back with hammers. In the lodge's burial register the
entry reads "U—, paid by a dove". The Gravedigger also hid the U under moss on
purpose, and admits it only when Unknown returns his lantern. In the cathedral,
the **Sister** of a choir that can no longer sing tells him that one of her
singers was called "the dove". In the choir loft he touches the dove's hymn
book and sees the choir in gold, a woman singing a list of names, and a burned
shape in the doorway (the cathedral's existing vision becomes part of this
thread). The Sister gives him the west door's key, and the city opens below.

### Chapter 1 — The Nameless Streets (12 rooms)

The old town runs on routines nobody understands. The **Crier** shouts news
from the night time stopped. The **Lamplighter** lights lamps that give no
light, one for each name he can't remember. The **Broker** trades in trinkets
that used to mean something. The **Clockmaker** keeps a stopped clock "ready".
Three threads run in parallel: the lamps, the Broker's locked cabinet, and the
clock. Restarting the clock for one minute lets the hour strike; the lower gate
to the drowned quarter is wound to it. Along the way Unknown sees the
Lamplighter's daughter's wedding in the market. He describes it to the
Lamplighter, who says: "That's *my* memory. How do you have it?" Unknown gives
it back, the lamp street keeps its warm light, and Unknown understands that he
is a thief of some kind. The cabinet holds a heavy seal ring that fits his
hand perfectly.

### Chapter 2 — The Drowned Quarter (11 rooms)

The canals have swallowed the lower city. The **Ferryman** poles the only boat
and takes memories as his fare. The **Washerwoman** scrubs the same shirt on
the steps forever; its embroidered initial belongs to her husband, a
sluice-keeper. The **Plague Doctor** treats sleepers in a hospice on stilts.
Their threads lead to the sluice house and up the aqueduct to the great valve.
On the aqueduct's dedication plaque, the seal matches the ring. The vision at
the valve is the petition, the signing and the striking, with the burned-out
figure holding the pen. When the burned figure turns, Unknown sees it from the
inside: it is him. He drains the quarter and gives the three their memories
back. "My name was Ulric. I was the High Magistrate. And this is my fault."

### Chapter 3 — The Hall of Records (12 rooms)

The upper city is his. His statue is headless; his edicts are on every wall.
The **Hangman** at the gallows remembers the man he hanged, and is kinder about
it than Unknown deserves. The **Puppeteer** performs *The Magistrate and the
Book* every night to an empty theatre. It's exposition and the chapter's
comedy, and it's how Unknown learns where the Ledger is kept. In the Hall of
Records he walks his own office in a **playable memory as Ulric**, and must
strike a name to open the vault, the Dove's. Writing it is how he learns what
he did to her. The **Registrar** waits in his chamber. He was the Ledger's
first entry, a test, and he made Ulric its last. He believes a nameless city
is a peaceful one. When Unknown opens the vault, the Registrar runs for the
belfry with the book.

### Chapter 4 — The Dovecote (10 rooms)

Across the cathedral's roof, among the gargoyles, the **Dove** keeps grey
doves in a dovecote. She is the only shade with a trace of color, because she
never stopped remembering him. She knows him at once. Her room holds the
letter he wrote and never sent. In the belfry the Registrar threatens to strike
every name again. Unknown doesn't fight him. He reads the Registrar's own name
back to him first, and the Registrar lets go of the book. Then Unknown rings
the bell and reads the names, the Dove's among them ("Elise", proposal). His
own is last. He gives it to the city, and the city takes its color back. The
epilogue is the grave at dawn, in color, with Elise recarving the name. The
journal's name field finally reads **Ulric**, as the shadow fades.

## 7. Tone

Dark, never dour (pillar 5). Unknown keeps his dry humor even as he learns
he was the villain; the jokes get gentler, never darker. The game never
excuses Ulric. It lets him make amends, one person at a time, and it lets the
people he wronged be funnier, kinder and more stubborn than he was.

## 8. Length (proposal)

- Prologue: 15 minutes.
- Chapters 1–4: 60–90 minutes each.
- Whole game: 5–6.5 hours.
